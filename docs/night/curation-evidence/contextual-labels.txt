"'t Suynighe Waertje" and "De Goelicke Waerdin"
   (Johannes and Lucas van Doetechum after Pieter Bruegel the Elder)

   under “Weather at Sea”  [caption]
   Two weathered faces meet across separate oval frames, their titles naming a fair weather and a good hostess. In an exhibition centered on exposure at sea, the print shifts weather into a human exchange: a condition imagined through companionship rather than an open horizon.

   under “Leaving”  [caption]
   The two faces turn toward one another from separate oval frames, held close by the print’s paired arrangement but never meeting. Their separation gives the exhibition’s moment of departure a fixed, formal shape.

"--And mist or glim, I'd sail with him, if he would sail with me--"
   (George Du Maurier)

   under “Weather at Sea”  [catalogue]
   Its title joins mist and faint light to a declaration of sailing together. The small, vulnerable terms "mist" and "glim" make uncertain weather the test of shared resolve.

   under “Leaving”  [catalogue]
   Its quoted line imagines sailing with another person, making departure conditional on companionship. The promise of going together sits against the uncertainty of whether that shared voyage will happen.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"-Ouriskoff!..trouvez-vous cela ressemblant?" from Actualities
   (Honoré Daumier)

   under “Weather at Sea”  [catalogue]
   A question about resemblance, voiced in French and framed as an actuality, turns looking into an uncertain act. The lithograph brings the exhibition's reduced visibility ashore, where recognition can be as unstable as a view through changing conditions.

   under “Leaving”  [catalogue]
   The French question in the title asks whether a likeness is convincing, placing doubt at the center of a person’s representation. In the context of leaving, the print turns the image left behind into an unstable record.
